Why this matters

When an advertisement looks legitimate—but isn't

Online scams can appear in sponsored search results, social feeds, and advertising placements. Payment for an ad is not proof that the advertiser is trustworthy.

🔎

Impersonation ads

Scammers may imitate the name, branding or support number of a company or public service.

🔗

Misleading destinations

An advertisement can route people to look-alike websites asking for payments, passwords or private information.

🛡️

Malvertising

Malicious advertisements can direct users toward harmful software or, in some circumstances, abuse browser vulnerabilities.

Practical protection

A quick safety checklist

These precautions can help reduce risk. No checklist can guarantee that an ad or website is safe.

01 · Don't assume “Sponsored” means verified

Paid placement is advertising, not a security endorsement.

02 · Verify the destination independently

For sensitive actions, type a known official address yourself or use a trusted bookmark rather than an ad.

03 · Treat urgency as a warning sign

Unexpected warnings that pressure you to call a support number, download software, or pay immediately deserve skepticism.

04 · Keep your browser and device updated

Security updates and protective browsing settings help reduce exposure to malicious content.

05 · Pause before sharing sensitive information

Do not provide passwords, verification codes, or payment details through an unexpected advertisement.

06 · Report suspicious ads safely

Use the platform’s reporting feature and your local consumer protection or cybercrime reporting channels.
